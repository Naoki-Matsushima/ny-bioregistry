    How an entry is built

    1. Find. AI-assisted search listed public databases for each product and role. Each was opened and queried for New York before use.
    2. Pull. Connectors query each database for New York and save the raw rows with a checksum and the retrieval date.
    3. Normalize. Rows are mapped to one schema (name, role, product, segment, location, evidence) and counties are assigned from Census ZIP areas when a source gives only a ZIP.
    4. Merge. Records are joined into one stakeholder when they share a federal UEI, or when cleaned names match in the same county at the same site.
    5. Trace. Every stakeholder keeps each source record it came from, with the record ID and a link to the original page.

    Direct and inferred entries

    Direct means the source itself puts the organization in the role, for example a fueling station the Department of Energy lists as selling E85, or an airport in the FAA's passenger statistics.

    Inferred means the role or product was assigned by a rule or an AI first pass, for example a wood business placed in the mass timber supply chain from the products it lists. Check the original record before relying on an inferred entry.

    Known gaps

    • Individual farms and woodlot owners are not named in public data, so growers appear mainly as sawmills and lumber businesses.
    • No New York mass timber panel manufacturer or SAF refinery appears in the databases pulled so far.
    • BioPreferred listings are self-reported; street addresses are left out because some are home addresses.
    • EPA facility records show registration, not whether a plant is operating today.

    Report an error

    Open the original record from the entry's source list. If the source is right and this registry is wrong, note the entry ID (for example NYB-0104) and send it to the registry maintainer.
